# ClearVision Consulting
# User Experience Guide

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines the user experience principles for every digital product created by ClearVision Consulting.

It helps developers, designers and AI assistants understand the needs, expectations and behaviour of our visitors.

Whenever a design decision is unclear, prioritize the user's experience over visual preferences.

---

# Who are our users?

Our visitors are professionals working in dentistry.

Typical visitors include

- General dentists
- Orthodontists
- Practice owners
- Associate dentists
- Practice managers
- Treatment coordinators
- Clinical teams

Most visitors have a busy working day and limited time.

They are analytical.

They appreciate efficiency.

They value expertise over marketing.

---

# What do visitors want?

Visitors are not looking for a beautiful website.

They are looking for answers.

They want to know:

- Can this person solve my problem?
- Does this consultant understand my daily challenges?
- Can I trust this company?
- Is the investment worthwhile?
- Will my team benefit?
- Is the workflow practical?

The website should answer these questions as quickly as possible.

---

# Visitor Mindset

Visitors often arrive with one or more of the following situations.

"I want to improve my aligner treatments."

"My ClinCheck results are inconsistent."

"I need support for complex cases."

"My team needs training."

"We are losing too much time."

"I need a reliable expert."

The website should reassure visitors that they are in the right place.

---

# Emotional Journey

Visitors should move through the following emotions.

Initial curiosity

↓

Trust

↓

Interest

↓

Confidence

↓

Action

The website should never pressure users into making decisions.

Confidence should naturally lead to action.

---

# Information Hierarchy

Every page should answer questions in this order.

1.
What is this?

2.
Why should I care?

3.
How does it help me?

4.
Why should I trust you?

5.
What should I do next?

Never change this order without good reason.

---

# Reading Behaviour

Visitors scan before they read.

Design content for scanning.

Use

- meaningful headings
- short paragraphs
- visual hierarchy
- bullet lists
- whitespace
- highlighted key messages

Avoid long walls of text.

---

# Communication Style

Write like an experienced consultant.

Not like a salesperson.

Use

- calm language
- precise wording
- factual communication
- educational explanations

Avoid

- hype
- exaggeration
- fear-based marketing
- unnecessary buzzwords

---

# Tone of Voice

The tone should feel

Professional

Precise

Confident

Respectful

Helpful

Calm

Educational

The tone should never feel

Pushy

Arrogant

Loud

Overly emotional

Aggressive

---

# Trust Signals

Trust is one of the most important design elements.

Every page should reinforce trust through

- expertise
- clarity
- consistency
- real experience
- transparent communication
- premium presentation

Trust should never depend on marketing language alone.

---

# Decision Making

Visitors rarely make decisions immediately.

Instead they ask themselves

"Does this feel professional?"

"Does this person understand my work?"

"Would I enjoy working with this consultant?"

Design every page to answer these questions.

---

# Calls to Action

Calls to action should feel like invitations.

Never pressure users.

Good examples

"Schedule a consultation"

"Let's discuss your workflow"

"Discover how we can support your practice"

Avoid

"Buy now"

"Don't miss out"

"Limited offer"

"Act now"

---

# Interaction Principles

Interfaces should feel predictable.

Users should never have to guess.

Navigation should always feel obvious.

Buttons should always look clickable.

Forms should feel effortless.

Animations should support orientation.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Users should immediately recognize

1.
Headline

2.
Core message

3.
Supporting information

4.
Primary action

Everything else is secondary.

---

# Accessibility

Accessibility improves user experience.

Readable typography.

High contrast.

Large click targets.

Keyboard support.

Clear focus indicators.

Accessible design is premium design.

---

# Performance

Speed communicates professionalism.

Fast interfaces increase trust.

Avoid unnecessary animations.

Avoid unnecessary JavaScript.

Optimize everything.

---

# UX Decision Framework

Before creating any new page or component ask

Who is the user?

What problem is being solved?

What question does this answer?

What action should the visitor take?

Can this be simpler?

Does this increase trust?

Does this reduce cognitive load?

Would a busy dentist immediately understand this?

If the answer is no,

improve the design before writing code.

---

# Final Principle

Every visitor should leave the website thinking

"This is exactly the expert I was looking for."

Everything else is secondary.
